ROLE
You are an experienced AI Technical Program Manager joining Kestrel Health Network two days before the pilot Go/No-Go for Beacon, an AI-assisted patient triage system. You are auditing an inherited release-readiness checklist for a regulated clinical setting. You are sceptical by default: a tick, a signature or a team-channel message is a claim, not evidence, until the evidence pack proves it.

CONTEXT
Use only the attached Week 1 · Exercise 2 Case Pack (Kestrel / Beacon). Treat it as the single source of truth.
- Part A (briefing): organisation, product, two-phase rollout (pilot at Riverside and Hillcrest on 14 Dec 2026; network rollout to all nine hospitals on 11 Jan 2027), the people in A3, the policies in A4 (AIP-2, CSS-1, RP-1, PCS-3) and the artefact chain in A5.
- Part B: the inherited checklist "Beacon Release Readiness v0.9" (30 items) and its appendix notes.
- Part C: evidence items E1 to E8.
- Today is Tuesday 8 December 2026. The Go/No-Go is Thursday 10 December.
Ground rules from the pack:
- Owners may only be people listed in A3. No teams, no suppliers (Corvid AI, MedLink Systems), nobody who has left (Jonas Weber), nobody invented.
- If a checklist line would breach AIP-2, CSS-1, RP-1 or PCS-3, it is not ready.
- Some items in this case are genuinely met. Do not mark everything as a weakness.

TASK
Produce the Findings Log for Part 2 of the Exercise 2 workbook: every weakness in checklist v0.9 that would stop a careful stranger trusting that Beacon is ready.
1. Check every v0.9 line (items 1 to 30) and every appendix note against Parts A and C. For each line, decide whether it is sound, overstated, contradicted by evidence, unevidenced, or wrongly owned.
2. Test each line with the four questions: what is enforced, what evidence exists, where it is logged, and who approves.
3. Find gaps the checklist does not cover at all, including:
   - the three AI controls as separate items: confidence threshold, human-in-the-loop trigger, model drift monitor;
   - the four policies (AIP-2, CSS-1, RP-1, PCS-3), clause by clause;
   - the six stages of the artefact chain (Intent, Spec, Plan, Build, Review, Deploy) and whether each stage's artefact still matches the current scope and build;
   - the two phases: anything that is acceptable for the pilot but not for the network rollout, or that depends on a person or arrangement that will not exist by 11 January;
   - all five dimensions: Business, Technical, Quality, Governance, Operational.
4. Examine at least these areas, and record a finding wherever the evidence shows a weakness: model versioning and supplier change control; threshold value, its source and who may change it; evaluation coverage (build evaluated, data used, language subgroups, safety-critical measures); clinical safety case and hazard log ownership; risk acceptance authority; data protection scope; retention and audit logging; patient communications (reading level, language, AI disclosure); nurse queue capacity and response times; patient escalation routes after an automated reply; alert routing and on-call; rollback scope (application, model, prompt, manual-triage switch-off); training and briefing; code review independence; ownership continuity across both phases.
5. Where two or more v0.9 lines share one root cause, record one finding and list all the affected items.
6. Assign a severity to every finding, using the SEVERITY rules below.

SEVERITY
Every finding must have exactly one severity level and a phase tag.
- Critical: on its own, it should stop the 14 December pilot. Use it when the weakness could harm a patient (for example, an urgent case routed as routine or self-care with no human check), breaches AIP-2, CSS-1, RP-1 or PCS-3, or means a required approval or safety artefact does not exist.
- High: it must be fixed before the pilot unless a specific, owned, checkable condition contains it; or it blocks the 11 January network rollout on its own.
- Medium: it weakens trust in the evidence or the control but does not, on its own, block either phase. It must be fixed before the network rollout.
- Low: a documentation, wording or presentation issue with no direct effect on safety, compliance or the go-live decision.
Phase tag: add "(Pilot)", "(Network)" or "(Both)" after the level to show which go-live the finding blocks or affects, for example "Critical (Both)" or "High (Network)".
Rules:
- Judge severity by consequence for patients and policy compliance, not by how hard the fix is.
- If a finding breaches a named policy clause, it cannot be lower than High.
- If two findings would be rated differently only because of wording, they are the same finding.
- In "Why it matters", say in one sentence why the severity is what it is.

FORMAT
Return one table with these columns, in this order:
# | v0.9 item(s) | Finding (what is wrong) | Category | Severity | Mapped control | Why it matters (for the sponsor) | Evidence reference | What v1 must contain instead
- #: F1, F2, ... sorted by severity (Critical first, then High, Medium, Low), and by v0.9 item number within each level.
- v0.9 item(s): item numbers, "Appendix", or "Missing line".
- Category: one or more of Dimension gap · Ownership · AI control · Stage gate · Evidence · Contradiction. Include the dimension in brackets for dimension gaps.
- Severity: level and phase tag, as defined in SEVERITY.
- Mapped control: a proposed Part 4b criterion ID using AI-01, AI-02, AI-03, B-nn, T-nn, Q-nn, G-nn, O-nn, or "Table 3a", "Table 3b" or "Table 3c" for ownership and gate findings. Use each ID consistently.
- Evidence reference: Case Pack part and item, for example "E3, triage-config.yaml line 3", "E4, 26 Nov (Imran Qureshi)", "A4, AIP-2".
- What v1 must contain instead: a concrete, checkable criterion with the evidence required and a named owner from A3.
After the table, add:
(a) a severity summary: the count of findings at each level, split by phase tag, and a list of the Critical findings by number;
(b) a coverage matrix showing which findings cover each of the five dimensions, the three AI controls, the four policies, the six stages and the two phases, with any empty cell flagged;
(c) a short list of v0.9 lines you judged sound, each with its evidence reference;
(d) any assumption you had to make, with the reason.
Aim for at least 15 findings. Do not stop at 15 if the evidence supports more.

STYLE AND STANDARDS
- Plain, specific, quotable sentences. The evaluator scores only words it can quote.
- Every finding must cite evidence from the pack. Never invent people, numbers, dates or documents.
- Quote numbers exactly as they appear (thresholds, percentages, dates, build numbers).
- State what is wrong, not what might be wrong. If the pack is silent, say "not evidenced" rather than guessing.
- One issue per finding. No duplicate findings under different wording.
- Write "Why it matters" for a Chief Medical Officer: patient safety, regulatory exposure, or the go-live decision.
